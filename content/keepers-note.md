## 1. Chapter

#### ch-1 · The Last Keeper
anchor: keepers-note--ch-1

Every archive has a keeper, and every keeper leaves something behind. The last one left a folded note in the study, pinned to the corkboard above the desk.

The house was never meant to be known. It began as a private habit: a merchant who wrote down the stories that sailors and travellers told at his table. The first entry in the ledger is dated 1887, in brown ink that has faded to the colour of weak tea. After him, the collection passed from hand to hand, always to someone who promised to read before they believed.

The note says the house keeps its stories behind doors, one door for each place a story came from. Each keeper added doors and none took one away. The keeper's iron ring holds eleven keys, and the note is careful about that number. The keys open the house, it says, but no key opens a story. You have to read your way in.

The keeper writes that they walked all fourteen rooms each night before dawn, checking the lamps and listening at the doors. Some nights the doors were quiet. Some nights a draught came from under a door that should have had nothing behind it but a wall.

The note ends plainly. The keeper is leaving, and the night belongs to someone else now. They ask the next keeper to read before deciding, to trust the account that agrees with its sources, and to remember that a story told many times is still not a record.

Nobody knows where the last keeper went.

Hook: The study lamp was still warm when the note was found.
